NFC credential · Patient App

What each layer does

The card and app provide access. The platform behind them connects clinical systems, verifies permissions and keeps a record of access.

ACCESS

Health Card and Patient App

NFC gives clinics a physical starting point. QR gives patients a way to initiate sharing from their phone. Later work would test signed summaries for low-connectivity settings and how to reconcile records when the connection returns.

DIGITAL HEALTH

Interoperable health records

The HL7 FHIR R4 bridge would exchange structured summaries with participating providers. Encrypted storage holds the clinical payload off-chain. The same approach could help connect in-person and virtual care.

See the integration studies ↗

BLOCKCHAIN

Kuiper Chain

A permissioned ledger would record integrity references and consent events such as grants and revocations. Access would remain scoped to each request. Withdrawing permission stops future access and cannot recall a copy already shared.

AI / FUTURE WORK

Help people navigate records

One possible use is to organize authorized information into a draft visit summary for a care team to review. Any AI feature would require separate evaluation, defined access permissions and review by clinical partners.

What the first build would prove

The first build focuses on synthetic FHIR records, app-based sharing, a card and reader prototype, and a queryable audit trail. A clinic pilot would depend on security review and local authorization. Regional integrations, genomics and consent-governed research would be evaluated separately.
